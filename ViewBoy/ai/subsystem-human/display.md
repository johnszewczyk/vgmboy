# Display

ViewBoy uses one responsive LCD canvas for the library, playlist, options, and playback status. Yoga lays out the screen; a four-shade framebuffer paints every border, selection, control, and bitmap glyph. Three flat grey Game Boy membrane lines frame the LCD.

The main screen has two centered toolbar rows inside a half-width wrapper. Previous, Play/Pause, Next, and Stop fill equal shares of the first row; Long Play, Repeat One, Playlist Random, and Library Random fill the second. Options is in the sidebar action row, and both playback rows disappear while Options is open. The sidebar has no section banners: its pixel-rendered search field and Library, Path, Queue, Favorites, History, Open, Sync, and Options controls lead directly into catalog content. The unfocused `SEARCH LIBRARY` placeholder uses the lightest visible LCD tone; active query text uses the darkest tone. Clicking the field clears and focuses it at the start, showing a blinking bitmap `|` cursor; typing filters catalog game/system names or catalog paths, and Escape clears the query or leaves search. Library groups games by system. Path lists indexed root folders and their folders/files; click a folder to expand or collapse it, double-click to load its tracks, and click a file to load its indexed tracks. The Path selection is remembered in local display preferences. The tree viewport clips and scrolls rows without resizing the sidebar or shifting the playlist pane. Wheel scrolling follows fractional LCD-dot deltas, so it can stop between rows. Bitmap `>` disclosure glyphs rotate in place as rows fold or unfold, using the shared animation timing. Child rows keep their full glyph height and slide through a clipped opening beneath their parent instead of compressing as the group folds or unfolds. Sidebar text rows are glyph-height; **Text Line Gap** alone sets the blank space between sidebar and playlist rows, independent of UI Chrome Gap. The selected sidebar row's shaded band extends one LCD dot above and below the text line. Selecting a game or path loads tracks from the read-only catalog bridge. Queue shows the active playback list, or the browsed playlist before playback starts. Favorites and History load their shared-store projections into ordinary playlist tabs with the same selection, close, reorder, and persistence behavior as other tabs. No pane has its own footer. The quiet app footer shows the active file path on the left and elapsed time, adjusted song duration, and playlist total on the right; Long Play and fade are included when the active backend supports Long Play.

The track pane begins with playlist tabs, followed by centered column headings. It has no separate catalog or queue title row. Selecting a game in the sidebar opens or reuses a named tab containing that game's tracks. Selecting a tab makes its list the visible playlist; while a track is playing, browsing another tab does not replace the latched playback queue. Each tab is one outlined rectangle with its left-aligned title on the left and an unframed `X` on the right; titles shrink and end in an ellipsis glyph when space is limited. Tabs use the shared auto-resize easing to slide open when added and slide shut when closed. Dragging a tab into a neighbor triggers the live swap after the pointer moves at most two LCD dots into that tab from the direction of travel; neighboring tabs ease into their new slots while the pointer is held, and changing direction retargets from their current positions. Releasing the drag eases the tab strip into the committed order, which persists in the native playlist snapshot. `[+]` duplicates the selected playlist in a new tab. One empty playlist remains available, and up to 64 playlist snapshots persist through ViewBoy's native preference store. Favorites and History are ordinary tabs sourced from the shared favorite and playback-history stores. Command-1 through Command-9 select the corresponding playlist tab.

The table can show the numbered `#`, favorite, file, title, game, artist, system, path, length, and size columns. Playlist text rows are glyph-height; **Text Line Gap** alone sets the blank space between playlist and sidebar rows. The selection band extends one LCD dot above and below its selected text row. **UI Chrome Gap** controls the separation below headings and between playlist columns. UI Button Pad does not add vertical space to playlist text. The first `#` column is fixed, numbered to fit the list, and numbers each displayed row. The favorite column is labeled `FAV` and shows the shared square checkbox, outlined when off and filled when on; clicking flips it around its vertical axis and updates the shared favorite store. Right-click any table header to show or hide File, Game, Artist, Path, Size, and Date/Time. Options exposes the same visibility controls as an unframed square-checkbox list under Library > Playlist Columns. Auto-Hide Empty Columns hides enabled metadata columns with no values in the active playlist. Every enabled column remains present in a horizontally scrollable viewport; trackpad horizontal scroll, Shift+wheel, Shift+Left/Right, and the bottom scrollbar move it. Drag header cells to reorder columns; moving the pointer at most two LCD dots into a neighbor triggers the live swap and animates surrounding headers into their slots. The easing continues during the held drag and retargets from current positions when direction changes. The numbered `#` column remains pinned. Column order is remembered in local display preferences. When Auto-size Columns is enabled, metadata widths fit their headings and available values within their column caps. Header and row widths ease together when the set changes.

Options retains its alphabetized table-of-contents pane and larger content pane. Settings keep their existing grouped controls; Library > Playlist Columns uses an unframed square-checkbox list. **UI Button Pad**, **UI Chrome Gap**, and **Text Line Gap** are the only spacing settings. Each adjusts from one to eight LCD dots. UI Button Pad sets control insets and standard row height. UI Chrome Gap sets surrounding interface spacing and playlist-column spacing. Text Line Gap controls both text panes and does not inherit chrome gaps. Spacing changes animate with the shared duration and easing, and readouts stay integer-width. Sidebar navigation buttons share their available width, and its search field follows the shared control height, padding, and chrome gap. The quiet app footer shows the active file path on the left and a right-aligned elapsed/song/playlist clock; song and playlist durations include Long Play time and fade when Long Play applies.

The Options screen uses a vertical alphabetized table of contents and one large content pane. Opening it moves the current screen down while Options rolls in from the top; Back reverses that motion. Its pages are Audio, Database, Display, Interface, Library, Methods, Playback, Theme, and Transport. Database shows the catalog location/schema/count, reloads the catalog projection, and exposes archive-cache location, usage, enablement, size limit, clear, and Finder actions. Audio presents ten horizontal equalizer bars, each at least 100 LCD dots wide and otherwise filling the pane. Every band uses the same label width, slider width and height, value width, horizontal inset, and vertical spacing. The `[-]`/`[+]` pairs sit together before each EQ gauge and before the Volume gauge. Each EQ bar selects one of 25 gain values from flat 0 to +12 dB in 0.5 dB increments and sends changes through the native audio preference path. Its empty left endpoint is flat; +12 dB fills to the right endpoint. The four-shade fill and numeric gain animate together during increases and decreases.

The Micro 3×5 and Standard 5×7 bitmap fonts are independently authored and switchable on the Display page. Both fonts use uppercase glyphs only; lowercase labels are normalized to uppercase before pixels are drawn. Display also selects LCD dot size from 2–6 device pixels per logical dot, defaulting to 3; larger dots keep the matrix more visible while reducing logical-dot count across the same window. The Theme page selects GameBoy or NightBoy palettes and standard or high-contrast ink. Both palettes map the same four framebuffer shades; High Contrast changes tone 0 (`#333333` for GameBoy and brighter silver for NightBoy) without adding a fifth display value. NightBoy also changes the narrow membrane lines to a dark-purple surround with silver details.

Each logical dot is a square device-pixel cell at the selected size, with a shaded face that leaves a one-device-pixel unlit LCD seam along its bottom and right edges. The grid scales with available space and device-pixel ratio, independent of the Game Boy's native 160×144 resolution. One Motion setting controls animation enablement and duration across selection, disclosure, column widths, tab reorder, EQ, spacing, scrolling, and Options. Its FPS cap is shared by every animation and defaults to 60; options are 30, 60, 90, 120, and 144 FPS. requestAnimationFrame timestamps pace Canvas updates. The FPS value is a cap; actual presentation also depends on display refresh and WKWebView compositing.

The renderer models the visible dot matrix and four quantized LCD tones, not the Game Boy's controller or electrical drive circuitry. Circuit-level simulation would not improve this app's screen output. Future authenticity work should focus on measurable optical traits such as dot aperture, cell edge, subtle viewing-angle shading, and restrained pixel response or ghosting. Keep any response effect frame-delta based and optional so static text remains crisp and canvas redraws stay inexpensive.

Click the sidebar toolbar to switch Library/Path modes, open a local path, or reload the catalog; click a tree row to expand a Path folder or select a game/file. Click a track to select it, double-click to play it, and click a header to sort. Right-click a header to open the LCD column visibility menu. Command-1 through Command-9 select playlist tabs; Command-Shift-D opens Favorites in its tab; Command-Shift-H opens Playback History in its tab; Command-comma toggles Options; Command-W closes the current playlist tab; Command-Q quits ViewBoy. Path, Console, and Disk Path views use their sidebar buttons or the View menu, Command-T duplicates a playlist in a tab, and Command-O opens a local path. F7/F8/F9 invoke Previous/Play-Pause/Next. Arrow keys move selection, Enter plays, and Space toggles playback.
